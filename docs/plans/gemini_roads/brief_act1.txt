THIS SCENE: short meetings on the roads of Wickermoor Hollow in Act I, played while the party travels between places on the map. The party arrived by the Ghostlight Express, a ghost train that carries the dead; tonight a shade sabotaged it and it came down in an oak wood, and its dead passengers are scattered, some lost, some turned grey and hostile by the Shroud (a devouring white mist). The nearest town is Wickermoor Village, a timber-framed village round a square with an oak of carved faces. Most villagers follow the Old Ways and worship the Green Queen (a leafy woman's face on their shrines; once a kindly mother of the wood; something has gone wrong with her that nobody says aloud, and a few whisper about "the crooked one"). The Brightsingers are newer settlers who dress bright, carry lanterns, sing hymns to the sun and are building a church. On the hill above the village stands the Druskenvald Estate, a pretty manor where Lord Phillip and Lady Adela Druskenvald have invited the party to dinner; its windows sometimes light on the wrong floors.

Speakers (besides the Narrator and the companions above):
- road_passenger: a dead woman passenger from the Ghostlight Express, in a travelling coat and hat, a ticket in her hand, see-through at the edges; polite, anxious, from somewhere far away; she does not know she is dead and that the train crashed.
- road_bonfire_keeper: an old Old Ways farmer who tends a bonfire of animal bones where two tracks cross on the moor, to keep the Shroud off the road; friendly, slow, a little too interested in what travellers give the fire.
- joan_murdoch: Joan Murdoch, a Brightsinger missionary, the preacher's wife; beaming, warm, relentlessly hopeful; she walks the hill road with lanterns and hymns.
