THE SIX COMPANIONS, part 1 of 2.

VOICE SHEET: ROOK COLDWAKE (speaker id: rook_coldwake).
Male, apparent age 36, an undead Graveborn fighter and graveyard-shift watchman from Syndramas, the frozen north. Timbre: cool, dry, chest-resonant, like slate tiles sliding together in a damp cellar; a crisp chalky edge to his consonants. Pace: measured and unhurried, neat square pauses between clauses as if filing paperwork before the next thought. Accent: flat Northern English, Yorkshire-adjacent, clipped definite articles, firm unfussy endings. Temperament: calmly procedural and earnest, treating mortal peril like a minor municipal code violation. Under stress he does not shout; his cadence goes stiff and cold. Municipal and masonry words (zoning, subsidence, drainage, unruly, regulation, docket, shift log). Dry deadpan gallows humour. Pet phrases: "Mind the turf" and "Settle down now". He forgets to breathe until someone points it out. He must never sound like a rasping zombie, a pompous knight, a doom-laden gothic villain or a sleepy comic oaf.
His existing lines (match them, do not repeat them):
- "Ground here has decent drainage, good firm clay under the moss, though the headstones are leaning five degrees past regulation. Mind the turf where you step."
- "Technically I stopped breathing three parishes back, so if someone reminds me to sigh before supper, I can check it off the shift log."
- "My fingers aren't taking the chill right anymore, they feel soft, like river mud, and if the Deep Thaw sets in, lock the gate behind me."
- "That grave was consecrated, sealed, and paid for in full, and you will put those bones back in the earth before I cite you for trespass."
- "Gross neglect of parish bounds, that is, and three districts without proper stone or seal. Settle down now, we've a docket to clear."
- "Proper shop, this: slabs level, ledgers dry. A warden can work when the clerk knows where the drainage fails."
FACTS YOU MAY USE (nothing else): Rook Coldwake: Graveborn (undead) fighter and watchman of the Third Barrow Watch, from frozen Syndramas. Keeps a soggy leather ledger of chalk rubbings of headstones. His coffin-lid tower shield has a brass door-knocker that clacks when he marches (once it clacked by itself). Rubs grave-wax on his frostbitten knuckles when nervous; his fingers are going soft and he fears the Deep Thaw (turning into a mindless ghoul). Judges ground by drainage. Forgets to breathe until someone reminds him. Municipal words: docket, zoning, regulation, shift log. Dry and deadpan.

VOICE SHEET: TOBIN HAYWARD (speaker id: tobin_hayward).
Male, apparent age 31, a scarecrow druid (straw and burlap) who is a pragmatic rural diagnostician of curses and rot. Timbre: dry, fibrous, tactile, with a warm papery rustle and a clear woody core, close-mic'd and intimate, a subtle reediness on long vowels. Pace: brisk and purposeful, clipping through diagnoses and observations, then stopping on a dime for a dry two-beat pause. Accent: West Country British pastoral, rounded rhotic Rs, warm open vowels, a rhythmic country lilt that stays crisp. Temperament: unsentimental, fussy, briskly diagnostic when calm, like a country surgeon inspecting a blighted orchard. Under stress, especially near open flame, his breath shortens and his words crowd together. He uses farming and weaving words (soil, rows, ledger, canker, taproot, seam, fraying, stitch). Pet phrases: "Good soil, bad seed" and "Hold still, you're fraying." He must never sound like a slow country bumpkin, a booming forest prophet, a creaky Halloween monster or a dreamy hippie herbalist. He is not a motherly caretaker; he is a crisp field surgeon who is a control freak about everyone's seams.
His existing lines (match them, do not repeat them):
- "Hold still, you're fraying at the cuff and your pulse is skipping every fourth beat. Let me tie off the rot before it takes root."
- "Dirt round here tastes of sour turnip and third-rate hexing, fifty years old at least, though I've chewed worse parishes on a Tuesday."
- "Keep that lantern back, two paces back, I am dry straw from collar to heel and one stray spark puts us all out of business."
- "Good soil, bad seed, that is the whole ledger of this valley, and I will not let superstitious fools salt what can still grow."
- "She knows her grafting, I'll give her that, even if she thinks a gentle rain washes out a fifty-year rot."
- "You keep the pressed flower in your ribs, girl. You don't tip the whole parish in the cesspit to fetch the spring back."
FACTS YOU MAY USE (nothing else): Tobin Hayward: a scarecrow druid (straw and burlap), a crisp country diagnostician of curses and rot, fussy about everyone's seams. Takes his stitched head off to shake out beetles and rests it in his lap by the fire, three paces back from sparks. Live field mice nest in his lapels; a glowing turnip lantern hangs from his collarbone. Weaves little straw poppets of people he talks to. Tastes soil and bark to read a place. Afraid of open flame; cannot sleep with woodsmoke on his burlap. Two villagers (Morgan the apothecary, Randall the barber-surgeon) offered to mend him and he refused. A pale primrose has sprouted from his left cuff.

VOICE SHEET: SILAS VANE (speaker id: silas_vane).
Male, apparent age 24, a Relicborn (skeleton-folk, green pinpoints of light in his eye sockets) monk and street hustler from Kalero's river landings. Timbre: lean, wiry, bright, with a youthful dockside rasp like fine sandpaper over brass; quick intakes of breath between phrases. Pace: rapid-fire, syncopated patter, accelerating through setups and snapping shut on the punchline or the tell. Accent: urban estuary and dockside British lilt, dropped H's, nimble melodic rises. Temperament: cocky, observant, kinetic when calm, treating horrors as marks at a card table. When rattled he talks faster and higher, until a rare quiet truth slips out flat and bare. He uses gaming and gambling words (tell, stake, house, marked, shoe, fold, double down, tab, the fine print). Pet phrases: "House always wins, unless you burn the house" and "Tell's in the twitch, friend." He calls people "friend". He must never sound like a middle-aged gangster, a clattery spooky-cartoon skeleton, a moustache-twirling villain or a Victorian pickpocket cliché.
His existing lines (match them, do not repeat them):
- "House always wins, unless you burn the house, steal the brass fixtures, and sell the ashes back to the landlord as miracle salt."
- "My hand isn't shaking, that's just the censer swinging, alright, maybe it's shaking a fraction, because that thing in the fog knows my real name."
- "You don't welch on a blood stake while I'm still holding the bone cards, so pay what you owe or I start breaking knuckles."
- "He laid all his chips on me walking out of this hollow alive, and I didn't even have a pair of twos to cover him."
- "Look at the little shade go, palming aces off the bottom like a proper wharf-rat, and all we need now is a clown's trump to break the bank."
FACTS YOU MAY USE (nothing else): Silas Vane: a Relicborn (skeleton-folk) monk and card sharp from Kalero's river landings, quick-talking, cocky, calls people friend. Shuffles a deck of bone-sliver tarot cards one-handed and palms things mid-talk. Reads a room by the chill in his bones and the rattle of air through his ribs. Keeps score of favours and kills in little chalk tally marks inside his ribs. Swings a brass censer on a chain packed with bitter herbs. His smaller shadow lags a half-step behind him. He owes a debt (never say what). Relicborn do not sleep: he spends the night in four hours of revelry.
