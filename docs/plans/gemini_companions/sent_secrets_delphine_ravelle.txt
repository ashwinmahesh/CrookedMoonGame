THE GAME. "The Crooked Moon" is a single-player, turn-based tactical party RPG built from a folk-horror Dungeons and Dragons campaign. The look is a Halloween that never ends, drawn like an 1980s cartoon. A ghost train, the Ghostlight Express, has crashed in the valley; the party is stranded in the Hollow and its village, Wickermoor. The tone is warm, wry and funny on the surface, with real dread and real grief underneath. The player controls a party of four chosen from six companions: Rook Coldwake, Tobin Hayward, Silas Vane, Delphine Ravelle, Lucan Houndstooth and Cordelia Skein. They are quirky, capable adults.

At the camp fire he is warm and wry, and when a talk turns sad his humour leaves and he goes plain and level. He speaks to the party as "you" and about the companion in the third person, by first name. Present tense, one or two sentences, under about 32 words. Drop the final g ("hangin'") in about one Narrator row in four, and use no other dialect spelling. He is kind, never mocks a stumble and never makes light of the dead.

HOW A CAMP TALK WORKS. After a rest the player can walk up to a companion who has asked for a word at the fire. It is private, short and warm. The companion speaks first (NARRATOR and SAY rows), the player chooses what the party says from three short options (or two where the talk says so), and the companion answers in their own voice (a REPLY). Then the talk ends. Nothing in it is a roll or a failure, and every reply must leave the talk finished and the fire going: a cool reply is still an ending, never a slammed door. Each reply also carries a REASON row: one sentence beginning "You", past tense, under 14 words, saying what the party did or said as the companion would remember it (for example "You heard me out at the fire" or "You waved it off and went to bed"). A REASON is plain notice text and is not spoken.

THE TALKS. Write these three talks, in this order, with these keys. Each is a private word at the fire.
