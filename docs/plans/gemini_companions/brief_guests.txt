You are writing short spoken lines for a single-player, turn-based party RPG on the D&D 2024 rules, set in a perpetual-Halloween
valley of farms, haunted manors and graveyards. Folks there talk with a folksy country drawl. The player's party is four heroes
chosen from six companions. Sometimes a story character walks with the party for a while as a guest: they don't fight, they just
talk. Write those guests' lines. Reply in Markdown only.

## How the lines are used

A guest's line plays now and then while they walk with the party, shown in a small speech bubble and later voiced. Each "moment"
below is one tiny scene: one to three lines from the guest, and optionally ONE reply from a companion (only if it lands better with
a reply). It plays at most once a game.

- place: said on walking into the place named.
- quest: said on walking anywhere while the story stands where it says.
- fight: said just after the party wins a fight, while the guest is along.
- idle: said on a long quiet walk.

## Rules for every line

- Short and spoken: 6 to 16 words a line, never over 20. Plain words. One thought a line. People interrupt, trail off, joke.
- Each guest sounds like themselves (see their sheet). Pet words ("darling", "friend") at most once a moment.
- Never use game words (hit points, level, quest, roll, spell slot, check, party, NPC, turn).
- No new named people, places or history beyond what the sheets give. Never explain a secret marked "hint only": at most a
  slip, a pause, a change of subject.
- Never describe what the player does or chooses. No stage directions inside lines.
- A companion reply uses the companion sheet's voice and is one line.

## The six companions (for replies only)

Their voice sheets are in the attached context files (cast-1 and cast-2). Use only the facts there; a reply should sound like their existing lines,
but much shorter.

## The guests

### 1. Phillip and Adela Druskenvald (two guests who walk together; write each one's lines separately)

Phillip: Lord of Druskenvald. Tall, thin, slicked black hair, pencil moustache, striped suit, a black cane with a silver
goat-skull head. Smooth, sly, generous with money, old-money manners, 1920s high society. Hint only: something weighs on him; he
gets quiet and tight around carved trees and faces, and he is very tired. Never say why.
Adela: Lady of Druskenvald, his wife. Short, curly hair, sparkling flapper dress, a planchette necklace. A clairvoyant who reads
fortunes from her tarot deck. Bubbly, 1920s slang ("swell", "the bee's knees", "Philly"), unafraid of ghosts, pokes fun at her husband.
Their story: the party just survived the crash of a haunted night train, the Ghostlight Express, and saved the pair. They are
walking the party to Wickermoor Village through dark woods and moor, where a gift (a manor house on the hill) waits for them.
On the way, wolves attack. At the village the mayor greets them at the great carved oak in the square, and they go up the hill.

Moments (write each):
- place_wreckage (the train wreck, in a wood in a bowl of land, where they set out): Phillip and Adela.
- quest_road (on the road through the woods, before the wolves): Adela teasing, Phillip smoothing it over.
- fight_wolves (just after the wolves are beaten): both.
- idle_road_1: Adela offers a fortune from her cards for the walk, in a line or two.
- idle_road_2: Phillip on money, manners or the house they've been gifted.
- place_village (walking into Wickermoor's square, before the mayor): Phillip goes quiet at the carved oak; Adela covers for him.

### 2. William Lodge (Foxwillow)

A tenant farmer in his forties, decent, scared and stubborn. He rode to Wickermoor for help because his farm town, Foxwillow,
is coming apart: a blight took the herds and barley, scarecrows climbed off their poles and walk the lanes wearing carved
pumpkins, folk hear an old banjo in the dark cornfield and walk in and never come out, and neighbours are burning each other
out in a feud over a mill fire. A scarecrow's soul dies for good if its straw body is burned or smashed. Hint only: years ago he
stole a silver coin from the chapel poor-box and never put it back.
He walks with the party from Wickermoor to Foxwillow and through it, including into the black cornfield maze, until the thing in
the corn is beaten.

Moments:
- quest_ride_out (walking out of Wickermoor toward Foxwillow).
- place_foxwillow (the town's lanes, smoke on the wind).
- place_lodge_farmhouse (his own farmhouse).
- place_windmill (the burned mill the feud is over).
- place_maze (the first clearing of the black corn maze).
- fight_1 and fight_2 (after a won fight; he's shaky but grateful).
- idle_1 (about the harvest that should have been).

### 3. Yorgrim the Gravedigger (Maidenmist Cemetery)

The ghost of an orc seer, the cemetery's sexton. Deep-voiced, slow, kind, formal and old-fashioned. He carries a gravestone
chained to his back carved with the names of his whole clan, whose souls were destroyed in a ritual he was tricked into: his
penance. A spectral black hound, Isolde, keeps the sepulcher doors while he walks. His lantern keeps the mist back. He asked five
friends, the Fallen, to keep five beacon fires: Ol' Jericho Sticks (a straw man who loved making children laugh), Sir Marius
Renathyr (a morning knight on a white horse named Dawn), Captain Briggsy Kratch (a loud pirate with a heart of gold), Lethica
Nightborne (the Nightmother, gentle face behind an owl mask, who loved Marius), and Farryn of the Greenwood (a satyr who gave
her heart to a mortal woman). Something twisted all five, and the party had to put each of them down earlier in the story, so
the party killed his friends; he knows, and he does not blame them, which is worse. Now the party relights each beacon with the
friend's final rites. Hint only: a tear in the world under the cemetery whispers to him in his clan's voices and he is cracking.
Never say he will break.

Moments:
- quest_beacons_first (he has just asked them to relight the beacons; on the first walk out).
- quest_beacons_some (after some beacons burn again).
- quest_sepulcher (all beacons lit; the sepulcher doors stand open on a hall of coloured fire).
- place_gravestones (the Endless Gravestones: rows that never end, always a fight).
- place_kings_rest (King's Rest, an old royal tomb).
- place_undercroft (the catacombs under the sepulcher).
- fight_1, fight_2, fight_3 (after a won fight among the dead).
- idle_1 (about Isolde waiting at the door).
- idle_2 (he hears something nobody else does, and changes the subject).
- idle_3 (to whoever is with him: thanks, in his way, for the rites).

### 4. Mrs. Oakum (Skitterdeep)

The butcher's assistant in Wickermoor, a strange, private woman in a butcher's apron, plain-spoken country drawl, practical.
Her pa logged and trapped the north-west ridge and worked the old Glitterdeep Mine when she was a girl. She guides the party up
the hill trail to the mining camp because her knees "ain't made for ladder rungs". Hint only: she is gone every evening until
twilight and she is terrified of being seen when "the hour turns" (she becomes a wolf). She carries salted mutton scraps.
She walks with them from Wickermoor's north-west gate up the hill trail.

Moments:
- quest_trail_start (leaving the village up the trail).
- place_hills_trail (the pine trail on the ridge).
- idle_1 (about her pa and the old mine).
- idle_2 (a slip: she sniffs the air, or snaps at a deer track, then covers it).
- fight_1 (after a won fight on the trail; she's oddly calm about blood).
